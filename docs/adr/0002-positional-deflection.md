# Deflect by where the Ball meets the Paddle

A deflection sets the Ball's course from the position it struck — the centre of a Paddle sends it
flat, the edge kicks it up to sixty degrees off sideways — rather than mirroring the angle of
incidence. Mirror reflection would leave the vertical course untouched by any Paddle, so a Ball
traveling almost flat would keep returning to the same heights forever: players could aim it
sideways but never along the Court, and a Rally between two good ones would have no way to end.
Choosing a hit position hands the aim to the player, and because the angle is capped the Ball always
keeps at least half of its speed travelling sideways, so every Rally eventually does end.

The speed that carries through this geometry is decided here too: the Ball leaves the Serve at 250
Court units a second, gains seven percent per deflection, and stops accelerating at 520 — a ceiling
that keeps a Rally chaseable rather than a race against a number no keyboard can answer.
